## 1. Language Models Learn to Improve Language Models / 语言模型学习如何改进语言模型

**Date**: 2026-10-16 | **arXiv**: [2610.23344v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23344v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23344v1)

**Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to automate AI research, including language-model (LM) development, offering a potential path toward recursive self-improvement. However, turning experimental feedback into a learned ability to propose effective improvements across models faces two challenges. First, training such an ability with real feedback is expensive: reinforcement learning (RL) requires evaluating tens of thousands of proposals, each potentially requiring a full model-training run. Even at ten GPU-hours per run, reward collection alone would consume hundreds of thousands of GPU-hours, motivating a cheaper surrogate. Second, surrogate feedback can become unreliable as policy optimization favors overestimated proposals when the proposal distribution shifts. To address these challenges, we propose LM2LM, which trains LLMs with RL to generate effective LM improvement proposals. To provide scalable feedback, we use a research world model (RWM), a frozen LLM that predicts proposal gains from real experimental records provided in context, as the reward model for RL. To maintain feedback reliability, we periodically execute current-policy proposals ranked highly by the RWM and add their measured outcomes to its context. Across eight autoregressive and diffusion LM development environments, real-execution evaluations on held-out tasks show that LM2LM enables Qwen3.8-27B to outperform GPT-6-sol, even when GPT-6-sol is given 300 real experimental records in context, while requiring 33% lower data-generation cost. LM2LM also generalizes to a model family unseen during training.

## 2. LY-GWM-RoboCasa-Human300: Prediction-Guided Action Selection with a Graph Dynamics Model / LY-GWM-RoboCasa-Human300 ：使用图形动力学模型进行预测引导动作选择

**Date**: 2026-10-16 | **arXiv**: [2610.23274v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23274v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23274v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

We present LY-GWM-RoboCasa-Human300, a policy that combines a Human300-fine-tuned GR00T-N1.5 action generator, an action-conditioned graph dynamics model, and a learned binary state-reward scorer. At inference, the policy samples four action chunks, predicts the future latent features and robot state for each, and executes the candidate with the highest predicted-state reward logit. Predictions therefore influence the actions sent to the environment. The evaluated system uses a 16-step prediction horizon and keeps all three components frozen during evaluation. In the local evaluation on 50 RoboCasa 1.0.1 tasks in the pretrain kitchen split, with 50 episodes per task, the system records 207/2,500 successes (8.28%), compared with 181/2,500 (7.24%) in the historical self-trained GR00T-N1.5 baseline. The observed difference is 26 successes, or 1.04 percentage points. The atomic group improves from 141/900 to 150/900. Separately, the RoboCasa maintainers re-evaluated the submission and reported 354/1,500 successes (23.6%) across 50 tasks, with leaderboard rates of 48.3%, 16.3%, and 3.1% for Atomic-Seen, Composite-Seen, and Composite-Unseen. The local and official results are reported separately; their difference is not attributed to a model improvement. Recorded local audits identify 212,700 changed environment-input chunks, including 28,223 in atomic tasks. We describe the actual training stages, implemented inference mechanism, evaluation provenance, and public reproduction entrypoints. These results document an active prediction-and-selection system and a positive historical difference.
