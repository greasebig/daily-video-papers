<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

**Categories**: cs.CL, cs.AI

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Beam is a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model with 501.3B total parameters and 23.4B active parameters, specifically built and optimized for coding, reasoning, and agentic workloads. Beam delivers frontier level reasoning, while requiring 3-4x less inference compute than many models of its class. ## 4. Reasoning on Call: Efficient Agentic Robot Control for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation / 按需推理：用于长时域机器人操作的高效智能体机器人控制

**Date**: 2026-11-19 | **arXiv**: [2611.26341v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26341v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26341v1)

**Categories**: cs.RO

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

Large multimodal models can support long-horizon robot manipulation, but frequent model calls incur substantial latency and token costs. To address this problem, we propose RoC (Reasoning on Call), an efficient framework that coordinates a low-level policy and a high-level reasoning agent through an online task-state monitor. The monitor combines temporal visual observations with ordered interaction memory to jointly estimate progress within the active subtask and predict its successor. These predictions guide event-triggered agent review for coordinated execution repair and plan revision. Verified outcomes govern memory updates and subtask transitions, allowing routine execution to remain with the policy while reasoning focuses on decisions requiring additional assessment. On RMBench, RoC improves task success rate by 24.0 percentage points and reduces token consumption by 65.3% compared with the strongest agent-based baseline. It also outperforms the strongest policy-based baseline by 15.1 percentage points in task success rate. Real-world experiments further validate its effectiveness across three long-horizon manipulation tasks. Overall, RoC supports efficient agent intervention and coordinated recovery across execution and planning for long-horizon robot manipulation.

## 8. Law at the Speed of AI: Critical Legal Infrastructure for the Machine Age: Autonomy Under Law Architecture (AULA) / 以人工智能的速度制定法律：机器时代的关键法律基础设施：法律架构下的自主性（AULA）

**Date**: 2026-11-19 | **arXiv**: [2611.25985v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25985v1) | **PDF**: [Link](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25985v1)

**Categories**: cs.CE, cs.AI, cs.ET, cs.MA

<details><summary><b>Abstract / 摘要</b></summary>

The global deployment of autonomous AI agents within and across organisations exceeds the governance infrastructure available to constrain it. Agent communication and authentication protocols, principally the Model Context Protocol and the Agent2Agent Protocol, are maturing quickly, but maturity at the protocol layer does not produce legal legibility at the governance layer. Runtime enforcement written by the deploying organisation fixes the resources an AI agent can reach, not the authority under which it acts. Legal legibility is the capacity of a legal system to recognise, interpret and regulate activity by mapping it onto legal personhood, property, rights and liabilities. Existing (analogue) legal infrastructure cannot supply it for autonomous deployment. The resulting deficit is bidirectional. It concerns the attribution of liability when an AI agent causes harm, and the allocation of rights, including data and economic rights, generated by agentic activity and by the AI agent itself as a source of value and form of property. This paper characterises that deficit as the legal attribution gap, a material and unaddressed risk to economies, organisations and citizens that is magnified in cross-organisational multi-agent deployment. The paper describes the Autonomy Under Law Architecture (AULA), in which AI systems and data assets are registered as legal objects anchored to one or more identified legal persons, governed by cryptographically tethered smart legal instruments and evaluated at runtime.
